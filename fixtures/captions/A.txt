❌ Things you should NEVER do in Japan

If you're planning a trip to Japan, there are a few mistakes that many tourists make without realizing it. These mistakes can make your trip more expensive, more stressful, and sometimes less authentic. The good news is that most of them are very easy to avoid once you know what to do instead.

One of the first things that surprises visitors is that Japan barely has any public trash cans. People carry their rubbish with them until they find a bin at a konbini or back at their hotel. Keeping a small plastic bag in your day bag makes this completely painless, so you are never stuck holding an empty bottle for hours.

Timing your trip is also very important. Cherry blossom season is beautiful but also extremely crowded and expensive. Visiting Japan in months like November or February can offer a much more peaceful experience with lower prices and fewer tourists.

Food is another area where many tourists fall into traps. Popular chains like Kura Sushi or trendy Instagram cafés are often designed for tourists. While they can be fun to try once, they do not represent the real Japanese food culture. Some of the best meals in Japan are found in small neighborhood izakaya where locals eat. These places are usually cheaper and offer a much more authentic experience.

Accommodation can also be a problem for travelers who only look at tourist districts. Hotels in areas like Shibuya or Shinjuku can easily cost $100 per night for extremely small rooms. Renting an apartment in neighborhoods like Ikebukuro, Nippori, or Kinshicho can give you much more space while also being cheaper.

Another mistake happens when booking flights. Many travelers automatically choose direct flights because they want to save time. However, direct flights can easily cost more than $1,200 round trip. If you accept a short layover in places like China or Korea, you can often find flights between $500 and $600. The travel time difference is small, but the money you save can be huge.

Transportation is another misunderstood topic. Many travelers buy the JR Pass automatically because it used to be a great deal. However, after recent price increases, it is not always worth it anymore. For many trips between cities like Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka, simply using a Suica card can be cheaper and much easier.

One last common mistake is landing at Narita Airport. While tickets to Narita sometimes look cheaper, the airport is actually far from Tokyo. After landing, you still need to take a train that can take around 90 minutes and cost around $15 to $20 just to reach the city. Haneda Airport is a much better option because it is only about 25 minutes from central Tokyo and much easier to access.

Avoiding these mistakes will make your trip smoother, cheaper, and far more enjoyable.
