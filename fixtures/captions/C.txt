If you're heading to Japan and have no idea where to start, here is the whole trip laid out so you can save it and stop stressing about the planning part.

Start in Tokyo. Senso-ji is best before 8am while it's still empty and golden, Shibuya Crossing is worth crossing once, and teamLab Planets sells out weeks ahead so book it early. For food, the Lawson egg sando is an underrated konbini breakfast, and Omoide Yokocho in Shinjuku has tiny yakitori bars for about 150 to 300 yen a skewer. For shopping, Don Quijote and the Pokemon Center are the chaos everyone films, and Harajuku's Laforet plus the secondhand racks at RAGTAG cover the rest.

From Tokyo, Kyoto is next. Arashiyama's bamboo grove, the Fushimi Inari torii gates, and the Kyoto Botanical Gardens are the three to prioritize. Eat your way through Nishiki Market and Pontocho Alley, and since Kyoto is matcha country, a day trip to Uji or a stop at Tsujiri in Gion is worth it.

Osaka is close by and worth a couple of days. Dotonbori, Universal Studios, and Amerikamura are the main areas, and for shopping BIOTOP, Shinsaibashi PARCO, and the secondhand stores nearby are worth the detour.

Nara makes an easy day trip, mainly for the deer park, Todaiji, and the hike up Wakakusayama. Grab the fresh mochi at Nakatanidou and wander Naramachi, the old merchant quarter, while you are there.

Hakone is the last stop, good for a night away from the cities, with its shrine, the volcanic valley at Owakudani, and a glass museum. Try the black eggs at Owakudani, book a ryokan with an onsen, and take the ropeway over the steam vents.
