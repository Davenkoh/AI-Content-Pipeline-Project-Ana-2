🇯🇵 How to plan & prep the perfect Japan trip for first timers

Planning a first Japan trip can feel overwhelming, so here is everything to know before you go, from when to visit to how to get online.

Each season in Japan looks completely different, so it's worth planning around it. Spring brings cherry blossoms but it's peak season, so avoid Golden Week if you can. Summer has festivals but it's extremely humid. Autumn has nice weather, fewer crowds, and the leaves changing. Winter is ski season and also when the seafood is best.

For the route, a loop that goes Tokyo for 4 days, Hakone for 1 night, Kyoto for 3 days, and Osaka for 2 days covers the highlights without backtracking, with Nara as an easy day trip from Kyoto.

A few of my favorite spots for a first timer are teamLab, Sensoji, Shibuya Sky, and Omoide Yokocho in Tokyo, Dotonbori and Universal Studios in Osaka, Arashiyama and Fushimi Inari in Kyoto, the deer park and Todaiji in Nara, Ghibli Park in Nagoya, and the shrine and Owakudani in Hakone.

Book your hotel close to a main train station. Japanese stations are huge, and you don't want to waste time commuting every day.

Book your activities early. Popular spots like Ghibli Park sell out months in advance, so plan ahead. If you miss the Shibuya Sky sunset slot, the night slots are still great.

Most locals don't speak much English, so it helps to learn a few key words and keep a translation app like DeepL or Google Translate on hand for menus and signs.

Get a Suica card loaded onto Apple Wallet and top it up with about ¥1000 to start. It's the easiest way to ride trains and buses around the country.

For internet, an eSIM is convenient and affordable for solo travelers, while pocket wifi is the better option if you're traveling as a group.
