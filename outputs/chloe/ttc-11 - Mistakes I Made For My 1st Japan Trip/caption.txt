🇯🇵 Mistakes I made for my 1st Japan trip

Japan was everything I hoped for, but I made a lot of small mistakes on my first trip that cost me time and money. None of them are obvious until you are already standing there. Here is what I wish someone had told me before I booked anything.

The first one was at Fushimi Inari in Kyoto. I took my photos at the bottom where the famous red torii gates start, then turned around like most people do. That spot is exactly where everyone piles up. If you keep walking up past the Yotsutsuji intersection, about thirty minutes in, the path opens up and you get long stretches of gates almost to yourself. It is free and open all day, so there is no reason to rush it.

The next mistake was assuming I could tap my card everywhere. A lot of smaller restaurants, shrines, and markets are still cash only in Japan. I got caught out more than once. Carry yen, and when you run low, use a 7-Eleven ATM. The Seven Bank machines inside the konbini accept foreign cards around the clock, which a lot of Japanese bank ATMs will not.

Around this point I also gave up on planning our group trip in a messy shared spreadsheet. I started forwarding my saved tiktok links, notes, and travel documents into holicay.com instead, and it pulled out the locations and organized the whole plan in one place.

Onsen were another surprise. I did not realize that many public bathhouses still turn away anyone with a tattoo, even a small one. If you have ink, book a private kashikiri bath or find a tattoo friendly onsen ahead of time so you are not turned away at the door.

Escalators have an unspoken rule too. In Tokyo you stand on the left and leave the right side open for people walking up. I blocked the walking lane on my first day without meaning to. The confusing part is that Osaka does the opposite, so people stand on the right there.

I also left my passport at the hotel on a big shopping day, which was a waste. Lots of stores offer tax free shopping for tourists, so if you spend about five thousand yen or more and show your passport, you can skip the ten percent consumption tax. You have to have the physical passport on you.

Do not lose track of the last train. Most lines in Tokyo stop running around midnight, and I got stranded once and had to pay for a long taxi ride across the city. Check the last train time for your line before you settle in for the night.

The last one caught me off guard on the bullet train. If you bring an oversized suitcase onto the Tokaido, Sanyo, or Kyushu Shinkansen, you are meant to reserve a seat with the oversized baggage space behind it. It is free if you reserve in advance, but you can be charged 1,000 yen if you just show up with the bag.

None of these ruined my trip, but knowing them would have saved me a lot of stress. Save this for whenever you book yours.
