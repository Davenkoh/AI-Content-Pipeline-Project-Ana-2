A first Japan trip is basically Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, with two easy add ons in Nara and Hakone. It is close to one straight line on the shinkansen, so here is the whole thing in one place.

Tokyo first. Senso-ji is worth it before 8am, before the shop street opens and the crowd arrives. Shibuya Sky is 2,700 yen online for a slot before 3pm and 3,400 yen after that, and the sunset slots disappear fast, so book them the moment they release. teamLab Planets in Toyosu needs booking ahead and it is scheduled to close at the end of 2027, so it is a now thing rather than a next time thing. For food, Omoide Yokocho is yakitori under the train tracks and it is mostly cash, and a konbini breakfast is genuinely good, not a joke. For shopping, Don Quijote is the late night chaos, Nakameguro is the calm secondhand designer version of Harajuku, and Mandarake in Nakano Broadway is floor after floor of anime.

Kyoto next. Fushimi Inari is free and the grounds stay open through the night, so go at 6am or 9pm and you get the gates almost to yourself. The bamboo grove in Arashiyama needs the same early treatment. Kiyomizu-dera pairs with the Higashiyama walk downhill. Kyoto is also matcha, and the real answer is Uji, about thirty minutes out by train, where Nakamura Tokichi is the famous house. In the city, Tsujiri in Gion does the parfaits and % Arabica in Higashiyama is the river view everyone posts.

Osaka is thirty minutes from Kyoto and it is the eating one. Dotonbori at night is the whole point, Kuromon Ichiba is where you graze, and Shinsekai is retro and slightly rough in a way that is very Osaka. Takoyaki is molten when it comes out, so wait before you eat it. Kushikatsu has a no double dipping rule and they genuinely mean it. Universal Studios eats a full day, so book the express pass or accept the queues.

Nara is a half day from either Kyoto or Osaka. The park is free and the deer will bow at you for crackers. Todai-ji and the big buddha are worth the ticket, Kasuga Taisha has the lantern path, and if you want to actually get away from people, walk twenty minutes up Mount Wakakusa for the view over the whole valley.

Hakone is the last stop and it is the one night away from cities. Do the loop: the ropeway over the sulphur vents at Owakudani, black eggs at the top, the shrine torii standing out in Lake Ashi, and an onsen ryokan at the end of it. Tattoos are still an issue at most public baths, so either cover up or book a room with a private bath.
