## Verified (with confidence)
- **Tsukiji → Toyosu (slide 2):** The wholesale market AND its tuna auction moved from Tsukiji to Toyosu on 11 Oct 2018; Toyosu is now the world's biggest fish market and hosts the tuna auction (observation decks). The Tsukiji "outer market" (~460 shops/stalls) stayed open as a touristy retail-food street. Slide "it moved out in 2018, it's food stalls now" + "the real fish market and tuna auction are here" = HIGH.
- **Arashiyama → Hokoku-ji (slide 3):** Arashiyama's bamboo grove is a short path, extremely crowded (judgment, widely documented). Hokoku-ji (Kamakura) = a Rinzai Zen temple with a ~2,000-stalk moso bamboo grove, quiet, entry 400 yen, with the Kyuko-an tea house serving matcha (+600 yen) overlooking the bamboo; mornings are calm. Slide "a calm bamboo temple in kamakura with a tea house" = HIGH.
- **Osaka Castle → Himeji (slide 5):** Osaka Castle's keep is a 1931 reinforced-concrete reconstruction with elevators + a modern museum inside (not an original interior). Himeji Castle = an original wooden keep completed ~1609, never destroyed, the largest/best-preserved of Japan's 12 surviving original keeps, and one of Japan's first UNESCO World Heritage Sites (1993) + a National Treasure. Slide "a 1931 concrete rebuild with an elevator inside" + "the real 400-year-old keep, a UNESCO world heritage site" = HIGH.
- **Nara → Miyajima (slide 6):** Nara Park's deer are numerous and become pushy/aggressive around shika-senbei crackers (bites/headbutts commonly reported), and the park is heavily crowded. Miyajima (Itsukushima) has free-roaming deer too PLUS the great vermilion floating torii of Itsukushima Shrine (UNESCO; the Otorii finished a major restoration in Dec 2022 and again "floats" at high tide). Slide "the deer swarm you and headbutt for crackers" + "free-roaming deer AND the famous floating torii" = HIGH.
- **Gion → Kanazawa (slide 7):** Kyoto banned tourists from Gion's PRIVATE alleys effective April 2024 (¥10,000 fine, multilingual signage) after geiko/maiko harassment (chasing for photos, grabbing kimono). Kanazawa's Higashi Chaya is a preserved Edo-era chaya (geisha teahouse) district, the largest of Kanazawa's three, and markedly calmer/less crowded than Gion. Slide "tourists are now banned from the private alleys" + "higashi chaya is a real geisha district, way calmer" = HIGH.
