🇯🇵 Japan most OVERRATED tourist ❌ spots

Japan is not overrated. A handful of the places everyone films there absolutely are. These are the seven I would not queue for again, and the ones I send people to instead. Every swap below is a real place with a real reason behind it, not just a quieter version of the same thing.

Start with Dotonbori, because it is the first thing anyone shows you of Osaka. Go and see the Glico sign, take the photo, then eat somewhere else. That strip is priced for the view. Twenty minutes north in Tenma you get Tenjinbashisuji, the longest shopping street in Japan at about 2.6 kilometres and roughly 800 shops, and it is stocked for people who actually live there.

Kiyomizu-dera is the one I feel worst about putting on this list, because the temple itself is beautiful. The problem is the approach. Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka are the most congested lanes in Kyoto, and you pay 500 yen at the end of it to shuffle across the stage with a few thousand other people. Nanzen-ji costs nothing to walk into. The main grounds and the Meiji era brick aqueduct behind them are free, and on most mornings you will have long stretches of it to yourself.

Nakamise is the 250 metre souvenir approach to Sensoji, 89 shops long, and it moves at the speed of the slowest person in front of you. Walk it once for the temple and then leave it alone. Yanaka Ginza is about 170 metres of Showa era shops that survived the war, roughly 70 of them, most still family run, and the Yuyake Dandan steps at the top of it are where locals sit for the sunset.

Universal Studios is the most expensive mistake on this list. Entry alone runs somewhere between 8,900 and 10,900 yen depending on the date, the Express Pass is a separate ticket on top of that, and standby lines on the headline rides still hit three hours on a busy day. Ghibli Park near Nagoya is 3,300 yen for a standard weekday pass. The catch is that it is reservation only and tickets drop on the 10th of the month, two months ahead, at 2pm Japan time.

Nishiki Market got loved to death. It is a 390 metre covered lane and the shopping street association now openly asks visitors to stop eating while they walk because there is no room to move. Demachi Masugata is the arcade Kyoto residents actually use, and Demachi Futaba on the corner has been making mame mochi since 1899. There is usually a queue there too, but it is the local kind.

Golden Gai is a lovely idea that gets expensive fast. Most of those tiny bars charge a seat fee of roughly 500 to 1,500 yen before you have ordered anything, and a few of them are regulars only. Ebisu Yokocho is a converted Showa market with about 20 stalls around shared tables, and a proper night out there lands closer to 2,000 yen.

The Lawson in Fujikawaguchiko is the strangest one. It is a convenience store car park that went viral, and the town has now put up a barrier and crowd measures because people kept stepping into traffic for the shot. Oishi Park on the north shore of Lake Kawaguchi gives you the same mountain across the water, with flower beds in front of it, for free, with no gate and no queue.

That is my list. Some of these will annoy people and that is fine, so tell me which one I got wrong. Save this before you book anything.
