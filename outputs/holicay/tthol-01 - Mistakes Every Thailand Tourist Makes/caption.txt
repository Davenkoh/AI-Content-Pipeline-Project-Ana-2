🇹🇭 Mistakes every Thailand tourist makes 😭

Thailand is an easy country to travel and a very easy country to get gently ripped off in. Almost none of these mistakes are dramatic. They are the small ones that quietly cost you a morning, a few thousand baht, or the thing you flew out to see.

Start with the Grand Palace, because it is the one place everybody goes and the one place people get turned away from. It is an active royal and religious site, so the dress code is enforced at the gate: shoulders covered, no sleeveless tops, and trousers or a skirt below the knee. If you turn up in shorts you will be sent to the rental stall to buy a cover up for around 100 to 200 baht before they let you through. Entry is 500 baht and it includes Wat Phra Kaew.

Right outside it you will meet the oldest scam in Bangkok. Someone friendly, often in clean clothes, tells you the palace is closed today for a ceremony, or for cleaning, or for the monks, and offers you a cheap tuk tuk tour instead. That tour ends at a gem shop or a tailor. The Grand Palace is open every single day from 8.30am to 3.30pm. Walk past whoever tells you otherwise and go read the ticket window yourself.

The elephant one matters more than any money tip here. Riding is still sold everywhere, and the training that makes an elephant carry a bench on its spine is the part the brochure leaves out. Book a genuine no riding sanctuary instead. Elephant Nature Park outside Chiang Mai is the best known one, and you spend the day feeding and watching them rather than sitting on them.

On where to stay, Khao San Road is a fun night out and a bad base. There is no BTS or MRT station in that whole neighbourhood, so every trip across the city starts with a 20 minute walk to Sanam Chai or a taxi into traffic. Book somewhere on the Sukhumvit line instead, or at least within a few minutes of any BTS or MRT stop, and Bangkok suddenly gets a lot smaller.

Money is the quietest leak. The bank counters in the arrivals hall at Suvarnabhumi give you some of the worst rates you will see all trip. Superrich is the one Thais actually use, and there is a branch downstairs on the B floor next to the Airport Rail Link, plus branches all over the city. On a few hundred dollars the difference is real money.

Maya Bay is the one that catches people who booked around a film from 2000. Swimming in the bay has been banned since it reopened in 2022, so it is a wade in the shallows and a look, not a beach day. It also shuts completely every year from 1 August to 30 September so the reef can recover. Go outside that window, go early, and know you will arrive at Loh Samah on the back of the island and walk a short boardwalk in.

The last one is just sad. Do not eat every meal in an air conditioned mall food court. They are clean and cheap and they are not why you came. Go to Yaowarat, the Chinatown stretch, after dark when the street kitchens set up on the pavement, and eat where there is a queue of locals.

Get these right and Thailand is one of the smoothest trips you will ever take. Save this for when you are actually standing outside that palace gate.
