🇻🇳 How to plan & prep the perfect Vietnam trip for first timers

Planning a first trip to Vietnam is harder than it looks, because it is not one destination. The country is over 1,600km long and the north, centre and south are in completely different weather at the same time. Here is everything I wish I had known before I booked.

Start with when to go. Northern Vietnam, so Hanoi, Sapa and Ha Giang, is best from October to April when it is cool and dry, though December to February can genuinely get cold. Central Vietnam, so Hoi An, Da Nang and Hue, is best from February to May, and you want to avoid the typhoon window from August to November. The south, so Saigon and the Mekong, is best from December to April because the wet season runs May to November. The one date to plan around is Tet, which falls on 6 February 2027. The whole country effectively shuts for about a week, domestic transport books out, and prices jump.

For the route, work north to south so you never backtrack. Three nights in Hanoi, one night on a Ha Long or Lan Ha Bay boat, one night in Ninh Binh, then fly to Da Nang for three nights in Hoi An, then fly again down to Saigon for two. Ninh Binh really deserves a night rather than being squeezed into a day trip from Hanoi.

I used to plan all of this in Google Docs and Sheets and it never worked, because most of my research came from TikTok videos I saved and then never opened again. Now I forward my tiktok links, travel documents and random notes into holicay.com and it saves the locations and organizes my notes for me in one place.

A few of my favourite spots for a first timer: the Old Quarter, Train Street, Hoan Kiem lake and egg coffee in Hanoi, Lan Ha Bay and Cat Ba island up in Ha Long, Trang An and Hang Mua in Ninh Binh, the Ancient Town and An Bang beach in Hoi An, the Golden Bridge and My Khe beach in Da Nang, and the War Remnants Museum, Ben Thanh and the Cu Chi tunnels in Saigon.

On accommodation, book inside the walkable centre. That means the Old Quarter in Hanoi, District 1 in Saigon, and the Ancient Town in Hoi An. Crossing a Vietnamese city in traffic can eat an hour each way, so sleeping where you actually want to walk saves you more time than any other booking decision.

Book the things that sell out first. The smaller Lan Ha Bay boats and the Phong Nha cave tours go months in advance. Son Doong, the famous one, is booked out through 2027 and Oxalis is already taking 2028 reservations, so if caves are your thing then Hang En is the realistic version at around 333 US dollars for a two day trip capped at 16 people.

Language matters more here than people expect. Step off the tourist streets and almost nobody speaks English. Keep Google Translate and DeepL on your phone, both handle Vietnamese now, and the camera mode on a menu is the single most useful travel trick in the country.

For getting around inside cities, download Grab. It covers cars, motorbikes and food delivery, and because the fare is fixed in the app there is nothing to haggle over. Between cities, fly. Hanoi to Da Nang is about 80 minutes in the air and roughly 40 to 60 US dollars if you book ahead, while the same journey on the train is over 16 hours.

For data, get a Viettel eSIM before you land. Ten to twenty gigabytes costs somewhere around 10 to 15 US dollars, and the kiosks in the arrivals hall charge about 20 to 30 percent more for the same thing.

Get these decisions right and the rest of the trip mostly plans itself. Save this one for when you actually start booking.
