❌ Things you should NEVER do in Vietnam

If you're planning your first trip to Vietnam, there are a few mistakes that almost every tourist makes without realizing it. Most of them cost you time, money, or the more authentic version of the trip, and the good news is they are all easy to avoid once you know what to do instead.

The first one is booking the big Ha Long Bay cruise that everyone books. The main bay gets hundreds of boats crowding into the same few spots, so you spend the day looking at other boats instead of the scenery. Lan Ha Bay, reached from Cat Ba, has the same limestone karst views with a fraction of the crowd.

The next mistake is standing at the curb waiting for a gap in traffic to cross the road. In cities like Hanoi the motorbikes never really stop, so a gap never comes. The trick locals use is to walk slowly and steadily without stopping or running, and the traffic simply flows around you.

This is also where planning matters. I used to keep everything for our trips in a messy google doc that only I ever updated, while everyone else just sent tiktok links to the group chat. Holicay.com changed that for me, because when I dump in my tiktok links, chat messages, and random notes, it saves the locations and organizes everything into one plan.

When you land, do not grab a taxi off the street. Copycat cabs with fake meters are a well known way tourists get overcharged, and even government travel advice tells you to stick to a licensed app. Just use Grab, where the price is fixed in the app before you get in.

Another mistake is trying to see the whole country in one trip. Vietnam is over 1,000 miles from north to south, so getting between regions by bus can take more than 30 hours. Domestic airlines like VietJet often fly those routes for around 25 dollars, which saves you an entire day of travel.

Do not eat only in the Old Quarter tourist strip either. The most visible restaurants mark up the same dishes you can get better elsewhere. Look for the tiny places with plastic stools full of locals, which is where the best bun cha and pho usually are.

Renting a motorbike to ride yourself is riskier than it looks. For most foreign visitors a home license is not valid to ride in Vietnam, which can void your insurance if you crash and get you fined. Riding with a Grab bike driver or hiring an easy rider gives you the same freedom without the risk.

Finally, do not change your money at the airport. The airport counters give some of the worst rates you will see on the whole trip. Withdraw from a normal bank ATM in town instead, and take out a larger amount at once to save on fees.

Avoid these and your first Vietnam trip will be smoother, cheaper, and a lot more like the version you actually came for.
