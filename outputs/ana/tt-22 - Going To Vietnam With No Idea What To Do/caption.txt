Vietnam is long and thin, so the whole trip is really just one line down the map. Start in Hanoi in the north, work your way south, and fly home out of Saigon. Here is the whole thing laid out so you can save it and stop opening twelve tabs.

Hanoi is where it starts and it is loud in the best way. Walk Hoan Kiem Lake at 6am while the whole city is out doing tai chi, then get properly lost in the Old Quarter, because that is the point of it. Train Street is cafes now, they seat you and then clear the gate when the train is due. For food, Bun Cha Huong Lien is the Obama one and the fried crab spring rolls are what you actually order, Pho Gia Truyen on Bat Dan makes you queue and pay before you sit, and Cafe Giang up the alley is where egg coffee was invented. If you want to shop, Dong Xuan is the big wholesale market and Hang Gai is the silk and tailoring street, where you should price three shops before you commit to one.

Ninh Binh is two hours south and it is the stop people underestimate. The Trang An boat tour is 250,000 dong for about three hours and route one is the cave route. Hang Mua is 100,000 dong and roughly 500 steps, which is much easier at sunrise before the heat arrives. This is goat country, so order the mountain goat with com chay, the crispy burnt rice that sounds strange and is not. If you want the same limestone karsts without the boat traffic, Van Long is the quiet version.

Hoi An is the prettiest stop and the one you will overshoot on time. The Ancient Town ticket covers the old heritage houses and the Japanese Covered Bridge, the lanterns come on at dusk, and the full moon night is the big lantern release on the river. Banh Mi Phuong is the famous one, and cao lau only really exists here because of the local well water. Hoi An is also where you get clothes made. Yaly and A Dong Silk are the reliable tailors and you want two fittings, so go on your first day and collect on your last.

Da Nang is thirty minutes up the road and it covers the beach and the big sights. Ba Na Hills and the Golden Bridge are 1,000,000 dong for adults, and from 2026 that cable car ticket is valid across three consecutive days, so go at opening before the queue builds. Marble Mountains is easiest if you take the lift up and walk down. Eat mi quang, the turmeric noodle dish that belongs to this region, and get banh xeo down the alley at Ba Duong. My Khe is the long city beach and it is calmest first thing in the morning.

Saigon is the last stop and it feels like a different country to Hanoi. The War Remnants Museum is 40,000 dong and open 7.30 to 6, and you should go early and go slowly, because it is heavy. Banh Mi Huynh Hoa is the loaded one everyone queues for, com tam is the broken rice breakfast, and Bui Vien is loud and cheap and one night of it is plenty. For shopping, Ben Thanh and Saigon Square both expect you to haggle down to roughly half.
