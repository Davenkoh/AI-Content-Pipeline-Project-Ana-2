Mexico is really two trips stitched together: the middle of the country for food and history, then the Yucatan for ruins, cenotes and beach. Fly into Mexico City, work down to Oaxaca, then fly across to Cancun and do the peninsula. Here is the whole thing laid out.

Mexico City is the one people are nervous about and then never want to leave. Teotihuacan is the day trip and you want to be there at opening, because there is no shade on that site at all. The Frida Kahlo Museum sells out, so book online days ahead rather than turning up. The Anthropology Museum in Chapultepec is the best museum in the country and you will not finish it in one visit. Otherwise just walk Roma and Condesa, that is genuinely the activity. Eat tacos al pastor from any trompo with a queue in front of it, get churros at El Moro late, and buy your souvenirs at La Ciudadela where the prices are fair and nobody hassles you.

Oaxaca is the food capital and it is a short flight or a long bus. Monte Alban is a Zapotec city built on a flattened mountain top. Hierve el Agua is the petrified waterfall, easiest to reach by colectivo. Santo Domingo has a gold interior that is genuinely absurd in person. Eat mole negro because this is where it comes from, tlayudas at night, and chapulines with lime if you are willing. Oaxaca is also mezcal, and the good version is a tasting room like Mezcaloteca where they actually teach you, rather than a bar crawl.

Valladolid is the best base for the Yucatan and much better value than staying in Cancun. Chichen Itza is about 697 pesos all in, split across a federal ticket and a state ticket at the gate, and you want to be at the entrance when it opens. Ek Balam is the one you can still climb. Cenote Suytun is the light beam photo everyone has, so go early, and Zaci is right in town if you want a cheap swim with no tour buses. Eat cochinita pibil for breakfast the way locals do, and get a marquesita from a street cart at night.

Tulum is next and it is two separate things. The ruins sit on a cliff over the sea and are best at opening. The cenotes nearby are the real draw, Gran Cenote for the turtles and Dos Ojos for the cave dive. The beach road is beach clubs with minimum spends, so if you just want sand, Playa Paraiso is public. Eat at Taqueria Honorio in the morning for cochinita and El Camello Jr for ceviche.

Isla Holbox is the last stop and there are no cars on it, only golf carts and sand streets. Whale shark season runs June to September and peaks in July and August, so if you are there in that window, book it. Otherwise it is Punta Mosquito for the sandbar and flamingos at low tide, Punta Cocos for sunset, and bioluminescence on a dark night if the moon cooperates. Eat the lobster pizza at Edelyn once, because that is the island's whole thing.
